## What everyone assumes

State the common explanation fairly and in its strongest form. Not a strawman.

If the widely held view is reasonable, say so. A piece that sets up an obviously silly belief and
then knocks it down proves nothing.

## What the data says

The actual numbers, with the source and the period stated.

Show the working. If you computed something, say what you divided by what. A reader should be able
to go to the same source and get the same answer.

Charts and tables where they help. Do not decorate.

## What's actually driving it

The explanation the data supports.

This is the section the piece exists for. Take the time to build the argument properly, and be
explicit about the causal chain rather than implying it.

## What would prove this wrong

The specific observation that would break the argument.

Same discipline as everywhere else on this site. If nothing could disprove it, it isn't a finding,
it's an opinion with numbers attached.

## What it doesn't explain

What this argument does *not* account for. Every explanation has a residual, and naming it is more
convincing than pretending there isn't one.
