## Corrections

A fact, number or verdict was wrong.

- **20 November 2026.** [The record](/process/#record): three lines changed on Jacob's rulings, and the
  count with them. The tally was 36 sealed predictions: 17 held, 13 failed, 6 in between. It is now
  37 sealed predictions: 17 held, 12 failed, 6 in between, 2 void. A void is a sealed prediction that could
  not be scored as sealed; it counts neither for nor against the record.
  (1) [GST rises](/economics/2026-09-08/). Was: "Failed. The 2023 and 2024 estimates would agree within 0.3 points. Result: 0.45 apart." Now: "Void. The 2023 and 2024 estimates would agree within 0.3 points. Result: not scored, because the current index changed its basket in January 2024, so that year's step mixes the tax rise with the basket change. On All Items, which includes GST-exempt rents, they were 0.45 apart, which would have been Neither; on All Items less accommodation, 0.086 apart (not a re-run, not scored)." Why: the two years cannot be compared as the
  prediction was sealed, because the index changed its basket between them.
  (2) [GST rises](/economics/2026-09-08/). Was: "Failed. Januaries with no tax change would show no step. Result: they showed steps as large as the tax, so no national figure for how much of the GST reached prices could be published." Now: "Failed. Januaries with no tax change would show no step. Result: the five sealed Januaries without a tax change (2019, 2020, 2022, 2025 and 2026) moved by amounts similar to 2023 and 2024, so the tax years could not be told apart from them, and no national figure for how much of the GST reached prices could be published." Why: the verdict stands; the old result words
  did not say which Januaries the sealed test covers, and "as large as the tax" said more than
  the test shows.
  (3) [Minimum wage or the Progressive Wage Model](/economics/2026-10-31/). Was: "Not scored. The fourth bet, that employment in the covered industries held up: no public series qualifies (THESIS section 4), so it is not counted above." Now: "Void. The fourth bet, that employment in the covered industries held up: no public series qualifies (THESIS section 4), so it could not be scored." Why: this bet was listed as not scored and left
  out of the count; the same rule now applies to it as to the first, so it is a void and is
  counted as sealed.
- **20 November 2026.** [GST rises](/economics/2026-09-08/). One sentence set "a 0.93 per cent signal" against "0.61 per cent of
  noise". The 0.93 per cent is the price step for a taxed item; the 0.61 per cent is the noise
  of the All Items index, for which full pass-through is 0.66 to 0.75 per cent, as the article
  says further down. The sentence now gives 0.66 to 0.75. Another said the method "says nothing
  about the four fifths of the basket that moves in January". The four fifths is a count of
  price series (32 of 162 hold still), not a share of spending, so it now reads "the four fifths
  of the 162 price series", and the heading "The 80 per cent I cannot see" is gone. Numbers
  changed: no result; the two figures quoted in those sentences.
- **20 November 2026.** [COE quota](/economics/2026-08-22/). The last paragraph said: "The thing to watch is the gap between quota growth
  and bid growth. As long as bidding grows faster, the premium keeps rising whatever the quota
  does." That is wrong on the article's own data, and it contradicts the article's finding that
  the quota lever works. The paragraph now reads: "Bids per certificate do not settle the
  premium on their own: in the nine years since 2010 when bids grew faster than the quota, the
  premium fell in four." The section's heading says the same. Numbers changed: no result; the
  count in the new sentence comes from the yearly averages of the same bidding records.
- **10 November 2026.** [HDB affordability](/economics/2026-09-19/) and the
  [scorecard](/scorecard/). Both said median household income crossed 9,000
  dollars a month in 2022. The two yearly readings are 8,904 in 2022 and 9,646 in
  2023, so it crossed between them; 2022 came from a straight line drawn between
  the two. The sealed test asked only whether a crossing happened inside the
  window, so its verdict, held, is unchanged. Numbers changed: the crossing year.
- **29 October 2026.** [COE quota](/economics/2026-08-22/). Headline changed from
  "The COE quota went up 62%. Prices tripled anyway." to "The COE quota went up
  62%. Prices nearly quadrupled anyway." The average Category A premium rose
  287 per cent, to 3.87 times its 2010 level. The same change was made in the
  home page's start-here box. Numbers changed: none.
- **29 October 2026.** [COE quota](/economics/2026-08-22/). The opening quote
  said a Category A certificate cost about S$30,000 "in January 2010". January
  2010's two exercises cleared at $18,502 and $20,501; S$30,000 is the 2010
  average ($30,405, in the article's own table). The quote now reads "In 2010 a
  Category A Certificate of Entitlement cost about S$30,000 on average."
  Numbers changed: none.
- **29 October 2026.** [COE lottery](/economics/2026-09-05/). Headline changed
  from "Two in three COE bidders win. It is not a lottery." to "Two in three COE
  bids win. It is not a lottery." The 67.1 per cent is a share of bids. Numbers
  changed: none.
- **29 October 2026.** [GST rises](/economics/2026-09-08/). Headline
  changed from "You can only see the GST rise in your water bill" to "Water bills
  show about the full GST rise. The national price index cannot see it." The
  same change was made in the home page's start-here box and on the
  [scorecard](/scorecard/). Numbers changed: none.
- **29 October 2026.** [GST rises](/economics/2026-09-08/). The summary
