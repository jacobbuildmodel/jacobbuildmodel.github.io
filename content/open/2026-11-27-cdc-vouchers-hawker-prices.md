Open question. Predictions sealed on 27 November 2026.

On 11 June 2026 every Singaporean household could claim S$500 in Community
Development Council (CDC) vouchers, half of it to spend only at hawkers and
heartland shops. It was the ninth round for all households since December
2021, and S$300 more is due in January 2027. Many people believe the stalls
answer with higher prices, so that part of each voucher ends up with the
seller. Asked in Parliament on 3 March 2026, the Ministry of Finance said
there was "no evidence" of that, pointing to yearly inflation for all goods
and services. Nobody has shown the monthly hawker prices around the launch
dates. My bet, sealed before I open them: hawker prices did not step up when
the vouchers were issued. Set against restaurant and fast-food prices, any
step in the launch month and the month after was smaller than 0.5 percentage
points.

## The question

**CDC vouchers and hawker food prices.**

(a) Explain why a voucher that can be spent only at certain sellers may raise
their prices, using the price elasticity of supply (how much more sellers can
serve when demand rises) and the incidence of a demand-side subsidy (who ends
up with the money: the buyer, or the seller through a higher price).

(b) Discuss whether CDC vouchers or untied cash transfers are more effective
in raising households' real purchasing power.

## The argument: who ends up with the voucher

**The seller keeps a share.** A hawker centre has a fixed number of stalls and
each stall a fixed number of hands. When every household in the estate arrives
with money that can be spent nowhere else, the stalls cannot serve many more
plates, so the price rises instead. Part of the voucher goes to the seller.

**The household keeps it all.** A family that already spends more at hawkers
and heartland shops than its vouchers are worth just pays with the voucher and
keeps the cash. Nothing extra is bought, so nothing pushes the price. The
voucher then works like cash.

## Push it to the extreme

Thought experiment: suppose the vouchers were worth S$5,000 and expired in a
week. Queues would double and no stall could keep its prices. Now suppose they
were worth S$5 and lasted ten years. No one would notice. The real vouchers,
a few hundred dollars spent over as long as 18 months, sit between the two.
The question is which end they sit nearer.

## The obvious objection: prices rise every year anyway

They do, and stalls may reprice at the turn of the year. So the test
does not ask whether prices rose after a launch. It asks whether they rose
by more than in the same months of the years before the scheme, 2005 to
2021, and by more than restaurant and fast-food prices did in those months.
The launches of January 2023 and January 2024 are left out, because the
Goods and Services Tax rose on the same days.

## How it will be tested

The data is the monthly Consumer Price Index from the Department of
Statistics: the row for hawker centres, food courts, coffee shops and kiosks,
set against the rows for restaurants and for fast food. For each launch, the
change in that relative price from the month before to the month after is
compared with the same calendar months in every year before the scheme. That
is a placebo test: the earlier years show how large a step turns up when
there is no voucher at all.

Two checks come first. The method has to find steps that are known and dated:
the quarterly changes in the electricity tariff, and hawker prices in the
two Januaries when GST rose, 2023 and 2024. And it has to be able to see a step
of 0.5 percentage points against the usual month-to-month noise. If it
cannot, the answer published will be that monthly prices cannot show a step
of this size, not that vouchers leave prices alone.

The design leans toward finding nothing. It sees only a step at the launch,
while vouchers are spent over many months; the price row includes food courts
and coffee shops that do not all take vouchers; and a rise shared with
restaurants is taken out. So a held bet is weak evidence that vouchers leave
hawker prices alone, and a failed one is the stronger result. Before the
seal, the method was run on invented prices, once with a step built in and
once with none, and gave the designed answer both times.

## What it changes

The Budget for 2027 will decide the round after January: how large, and
whether as vouchers or as cash. If prices stepped up, part of each voucher
reaches sellers, which counts for cash or for tighter targeting. If they did
not, the worry about prices falls away and the case for vouchers rests on
what they do for hawkers and heartland shops. This page evaluates a policy.
It is not a guide to where anyone spends their vouchers.

## What would change my mind

- An average step of 0.5 percentage points or more at the five launches.
- A step that shows against restaurants alone, against fast food alone and
  against all consumer prices.
- A January 2027 step larger than any January from 2006 to 2021.
