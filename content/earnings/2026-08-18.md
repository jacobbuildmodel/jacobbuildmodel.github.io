> Baidu's results looked like a massive beat this morning. They weren't. Someone compared Chinese
> yuan figures against US dollar estimates, which makes any company look like a hero. The quarter
> was actually a small miss. The interesting part is what's happening inside the business.

## What happened

Baidu reported its second quarter. If you saw the numbers flash past on a data feed, you may have
seen revenue "beating" expectations by nearly six times and profit beating by nearly four times.

Neither happened. The feed was comparing Baidu's numbers in Chinese yuan against analyst estimates
in US dollars. One US dollar is worth roughly seven yuan, so the Chinese figures look about seven
times bigger than they should. It's the same mistake as comparing a price in yen to a price in
pounds and declaring one a bargain.

Corrected to the same currency, Baidu slightly missed. Revenue came in about 3% below what analysts
expected. Profit came in around 25% below. Revenue also shrank about 4% compared to the same quarter
last year.

So: a small miss, and a business that's gently shrinking overall.

## The part worth paying attention to

Baidu is really two businesses wearing one name.

The old business is search and advertising. That's most of the company, and it's slowly declining as
Chinese consumers spend more time inside apps than on the open web.

The new business is renting out computing power for artificial intelligence. Companies that want to
build AI models need enormous amounts of specialised chips, and few can afford to buy them outright.
So they rent. Baidu owns the chips and rents them out.

That rental business grew 50% compared to last year. Inside it, the specific part that rents AI chips
grew 283%.

The second number matters more than it first appears. Three months ago, that same business was
growing at 184%. So the growth rate itself is speeding up. A business growing 50% a year is doing
well. A business whose growth rate climbed from 184% to 283% in three months is running into demand
it wasn't expecting.

That's the signal buried under a headline that read like a disappointment.

## What management said about the months ahead

Baidu didn't attach specific revenue or profit targets for next quarter to its release. Management
pointed to continued investment in AI infrastructure without putting numbers against it.

One thing worth knowing: Baidu is a Chinese company listed in the US, which means it files different
and less detailed paperwork with American regulators than a US company would. Every figure above
comes from Baidu's own announcement rather than an audited regulatory filing. That's normal for
Chinese listings. It also means these are management's account of the quarter rather than an
independently checked one.

## Key highlights

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue vs expectations | About 3% below |
| Revenue vs last year | Down about 4% |
| Profit vs expectations | About 25% below |
| AI cloud rental growth | Up 50% on last year |
| AI chip rental growth | Up 283%, accelerating from 184% last quarter |

## What this makes worth watching

Most people tracking the AI buildout watch American companies: the chipmakers, the big cloud
providers, the data centre operators. Baidu's numbers are a reminder that Chinese demand for AI
computing runs on its own schedule and its own reporting calendar.

That raises questions worth following rather than acting on.

**Is this a China story or a global one?** If AI chip demand is accelerating in China at the same
time it's strong in the US, that points to genuine worldwide scarcity rather than one region's
enthusiasm. The next round of American cloud and chipmaker results is where that gets tested.

**Is the acceleration real, or borrowed from next quarter?** A jump from 184% to 283% could be real
demand, or it could be customers rushing to lock in capacity before prices rise. Those two look
identical over one quarter and completely different over three.

**What we're watching for.** If AI chip rental growth stays above roughly 250% next quarter, the
acceleration is real. If it falls back toward 184% or below, this quarter was customers pulling
orders forward rather than a lasting shift. Baidu reports again in roughly three months.

## What we couldn't check

Baidu hasn't filed these results with US regulators yet, so everything here comes from the company's
own announcement. Management's breakdown of the AI cloud business stops at a fairly high level, so
how profitable that fast growth actually is remains unclear.

Analyst attention has also been thin lately. Two analysts updated their price expectations in the
three months before this report, against eighteen over the past year. Fewer people are watching this
company closely than its size would suggest.
