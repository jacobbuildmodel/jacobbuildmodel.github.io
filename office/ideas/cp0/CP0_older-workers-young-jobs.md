# CP0 note 1: Older workers and job opportunities for the young

Input for the purpose review of 6 Nov 2026 (idea scout candidate 1). Not a verdict; no sealed
wording, no confidences.

**Stem.** On 26 October 2026 the Government accepted the six recommendations of the Tripartite
Workgroup on Senior Employment, including a retirement age of 65 and a re-employment age of 70
from 1 January 2029, a year early (MOM, 26 Oct 2026). The Senior Employment Credit, a wage offset
of up to 7 per cent for Singaporeans aged 60 and above earning under S$4,000 a month, gives way to
a Re-Employment Credit from 2028, and the Part-Time Re-employment Grant to a Flexi-Load Programme
(Minister of State Dinesh Vasu Dash, 26 Oct 2026). Details are due in the first half of 2027.

**(a)** Explain why raising the re-employment age need not reduce job opportunities for young
workers, using the lump-of-labour fallacy and the difference between workers who are substitutes
and complements.

**(b)** Discuss whether wage-offset subsidies such as the Senior Employment Credit or job-redesign
grants are more effective in raising older workers' employment without crowding out young workers.

**Decision.** MOM and the tripartite partners, designing the 2029 step and both successor schemes
(H1 2027); MOF, funding them in Budget 2027. If young employment fell after past steps: pair the
2029 step with help for young hires, or tie the new credit to net hiring. If not: the objection
falls away, and the debate moves to which instrument buys more older employment per dollar.
Stakes Medium-High.

**Rivals.**
- Young workers online: "We share the same pool" (The Independent Singapore, 28 Oct 2026, as
  cited by the scout).
- TWG-SE: "positive employment outcomes observed following previous increases" (26 Oct 2026), for
  older workers only.
- Gruber, Milligan and Wise (NBER w14647, 2009): in twelve countries, more older employment did
  not mean less youth employment.
- Boeri, Garibaldi and Moen (Journal of Population Economics, 2022): Italy's sudden 2011 rise
  displaced mainly the middle-aged; about one young worker per five locked in.
- Already stated? For older workers, yes (TWG-SE). For the young in Singapore, no source found.

**Data (labels and coverage only).** MOM, Resident Employment Rate by Age and Sex, annual, June
(first year unchecked: the page returned 403). SingStat M182201, participation by age and sex, end
June, 1990-2025 (bands 15-19 to 70-74). SingStat M183401, unemployment by sex, age and
qualification, 1992-2025 (15-24, 25-29, 30-39, 40-49, 50-59, 60 and over). Steps: 1 Jan 2012
(re-employment to 65), 1 Jul 2017 (67), 1 Jul 2022 (63/68), 1 Jul 2026 (64/69, first seen in the
June 2027 survey). Scheme parameters by year: to compile from MOM releases.

**Method.** (a) Event study of employment at 15-24 and 25-29 around the three past steps, against
each band's pre-step trend, with 40-49 as the comparison band (the Italian study's substitutes).
Three annual events support a precise "smaller than X", not a causal "yes"; 2022 overlaps the
post-COVID rebound and is reported on its own. (b) Singapore's data cannot separate the two
instruments, which have run side by side, so (b) rests on a reference class: an LLM extracts
effect sizes from evaluations abroad of older-worker wage subsidies and job-redesign grants, each
row hand-checked against the paper's own table.

**Smallest effect that matters.** 1 percentage point of the 25-29 employment rate against trend,
within two surveys of a step.

**Positive control.** The step must bind: participation at 60-64 rises after 2012, and at 65-69
after 2017 and 2022. If not, the young cannot be tested.

**G3 grid.** Yes = young employment fell against trend after the steps. Prior in per cent, not a
confidence.

| | Data says yes | Data says no |
|---|---|---|
| People believe it | "Seniors stayed and young jobs fell: substitutes" (15) | "No lump of labour: young jobs held as seniors stayed" (55) |
| Nobody suspects it | hidden link (5) | no news (25) |

**Seal-by and kill.** Seal by 15 Jan 2027, before Budget 2027. Kill: MOM or the TWG-SE publishes
a young-worker comparison around the steps before then (the answer is public).

**Cost.** 6-8 working days.
