# IDEA SCOUT 2026-11 -- candidate questions on Singapore decisions due by end-2027

Written 4-5 November 2026 by a cloud scout session, for the WEBSITE moderator's
purpose review. This file only scouts. It proposes no confidence, writes no
sealed wording, and grades nothing: the gates below are first-pass notes for
the Purpose Reviewer to test, not verdicts (office/PURPOSE_PRINCIPLES.md,
section 4: the chat that builds a piece never grades its own purpose).

## How to read this file

- Ten candidates, in no particular order of merit. The ranked top five is the
  last section.
- Each one follows the same shape: the claim as people say it, the decision,
  the testable question, the data, the six gates (G0-G5), overlap with the
  portfolio, the idea borrowed, and a seal note.
- **Seal discipline.** No outcome series was downloaded, opened or computed.
  Data checks used only metadata: the SingStat TableBuilder metadata endpoint
  (`/api/table/metadata/<id>`: title, frequency, first and last period, row
  labels) and the data.gov.sg v2 dataset metadata endpoint (name, coverage,
  column names). Neither returns values. Where a news article or a
  parliamentary answer has already published a candidate's answer, the seal
  note says so, and no values are repeated here. The section "Seal-discipline
  log" lists what the scout saw by accident while reading the news.
- Every claim has a dated source from the last 12 months unless marked older.
  Quotes are as printed; most sources were read as search extracts, not full
  pages.

## 1. Older workers staying on: do the young pay for it?

**Claim, as people say it.** Young commenters said raising the retirement age
would "just take away more jobs from the younger generation. ... We share the
same pool; I hope people don't forget about this." (Reddit, quoted by The
Independent Singapore, 28 Oct 2026,
https://theindependent.sg/singaporeans-share-mixed-reactions-about-new-retirement-age-re-employment-age).
Earlier: "We're competing with retirement-age people now" (Reddit post of
29 Jun 2026, The Independent Singapore, 11 Jul 2026,
https://theindependent.sg/we-re-competing-with-retirement-age-people-now-worker-alarmed-after-new-hire-turned-out-to-be-woman-in-her-60s).
The rival view: the Tripartite Workgroup on Senior Employment recommended
retirement and re-employment ages of 65 and 70 from 1 Jan 2029 "given the
positive employment outcomes observed following previous increases"
(TWG-SE report, letter dated 26 Oct 2026,
https://www.mom.gov.sg/-/media/MOM/Documents/press-releases/2026/Annex%20-%20TWG-SE%20Report).
That report speaks for older workers only; it does not address the young.

**Decision.** The Government accepted all six TWG-SE recommendations
(MOM, 26 Oct 2026). Details of the measures are due "in the first half of next
year", i.e. H1 2027. The Senior Employment Credit is extended only to
December 2027 (MOM, 3 Mar 2026,
https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2026/0303-partnering-businesses-and-workers-to-thrive-in-a-changed-world),
so its renewal falls in Budget 2027 or 2028. CPF rates for workers aged above
55 to 65 rise again on 1 Jan 2027 (CPF Board,
https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/infohub/news/cpf-related-announcements/new-contribution-rates).

**Testable question.** When Singapore raised the retirement age, did younger
workers lose out?

**Data.**
- MOM Manpower Research and Statistics Department (MRSD), "Resident Employment
  Rate By Age and Sex", Labour Force in Singapore time-series table, annual
  (June), released 29 Jan 2026 (https://stats.mom.gov.sg/Pages/EmploymentTimeSeries.aspx).
  First year not checked.
- SingStat M182201, "Resident Labour Force Participation Rate Aged 15 Years And
  Over By Age And Sex, End June", annual, 1990-2025 (rows include 20-24, 25-29,
  60-64, 65-69).
- SingStat M183401, "Average Resident Unemployment Rate By Sex, Age And Highest
  Qualification Attained", annual, 1992-2025 (rows include 15-24, 25-29).
- Event dates: re-employment introduced 1 Jan 2012; 62/67 on 1 Jul 2017; 63/68
  on 1 Jul 2022; 64/69 on 1 Jul 2026. The 2026 step is first visible in the
  June 2027 survey (MOM's advance release has come out in late November).

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: yes, as co-movement of young and old employment around three
  dated steps; not as clean causation. Outcome-neutral check: employment at
  60-64 must rise after each step, or the step did not bind.
- G1 Decision: MOM and tripartite partners writing the 2029 rules (H1 2027);
  MOF on renewing the Senior Employment Credit. Yes (young lose): pair the 2029
  step with support for young hires. No: removes the objection young workers
  raise most. Unclear: the record cannot separate the two. Stakes Medium-High.
- G2 Live argument: The Independent Singapore 11 Jul and 28 Oct 2026, both
  relaying Reddit threads; no forum letter or PQ making the claim was found, so
  the argument is mostly online. Timing: H1 2027 details; Budget 2027.
- G3 Headlines: yes, "Older workers stayed on. The young paid for it"
  (confirms a contested belief); no, "Older workers stayed. The young did not
  pay" (myth-busting a named belief). Smallest effect worth reporting: the
  reviewer sets it, for example about one point of the 25-29 employment rate
  against trend at a step.
- G4 Who it serves: readers aged 20-34 who feel they compete with seniors; MOM
  Workplace Policy and Strategy Division (Retirement and Re-employment Act) and
  MRSD; channel: MOM's H1 2027 release and the Budget 2027 debate.
- G5 Cost: 5-7 working days (annual series, three steps, a placebo age band).
  Displaces the menu's "next Hong Kong" candidate for the Budget 2027 slot: both
  ask who gets the jobs, and this one has the nearer decision.

**Overlap.** New. Shares the labour-market beat with the PWM piece.

**Borrowed.** Gruber, Milligan and Wise, "Social Security Programs and
Retirement Around the World: The Relationship to Youth Employment", NBER
w14647 (2009) and the 2010 volume (https://www.nber.org/papers/w14647): twelve
countries, testing whether older workers' employment crowds out the young by
tracking both around changes in retirement rules.

**Seal note.** No young-worker result seen in any source. TWG-SE's claim covers
older workers only.

## 2. CDC vouchers: do hawker prices jump when they land?

**Claim, as people say it.** Mr Lee Hong Chuang (Jurong East-Bukit Batok) asked
"whether giving out CDC vouchers to every Singaporean household regardless of
socioeconomic status results in worsening cost-of-living due to demand-pull
inflation" (oral answer, 3 Mar 2026, https://openhansard.sg/reports/oral-answer-4063).
The reply, from Senior Parliamentary Secretary Shawn Huang: "There is no
evidence that the CDC Vouchers Scheme has worsened the cost-of-living due to
inflation", citing annual all-items inflation. The street version: "If
anything, CDC only makes the inflation worse." (HardwareZone forum, 1 Apr 2026,
https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/lianhe-zaobao-sm-lee-vouchers-better-help-people-associate-them-with-the-purpose-of-assistance.7196894/page-2).

**Decision.** Budget 2027 (Feb 2027), MOF: whether to give another tranche and
how much. The tranche planned for January 2027 was brought forward to
11 Jun 2026 (CDC media release, 11 Jun 2026,
https://www.cdc.gov.sg/files/1__CDC_Vouchers_Scheme_2026__June__Media_Release.pdf),
so the next one is an open Budget 2027 decision. MOF in the same answer: "The
Government regularly reviews the effectiveness of our schemes."

**Testable question.** Do hawker prices jump in the month CDC vouchers arrive?

**Data.**
- SingStat M213751, "Consumer Price Index (CPI), 2024 As Base Year, Monthly",
  table span 1961 Jan-2026 Sep, updated 23 Oct 2026. Rows 1.11.3 "Hawker
  Centres, And Food Courts, Coffee Shops & Kiosks" and 1.11.3.1 "Hawker
  Centres". Start dates of these rows not checked. Comparison rows (restaurant
  meals, supermarket food) to be fixed before the seal.
- Event dates: nine digital tranches, Dec 2021 to Jun 2026 (the 11 Jun 2026
  release calls June 2026 the ninth). Earlier dates to be compiled from
  cdc.gov.sg releases. Recent ones: Jan 2025, May 2025, Jan 2026, Jun 2026
  (CNA, 11 Jun 2026).
- Design point: test on the mid-year tranches. Januaries are confounded: the
  site's own GST piece found January steps as large as a tax rise in years with
  no tax change.

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: yes, a monthly index against dated events. Outcome-neutral
  check: the method must see the known January step every year; if it cannot,
  it cannot see a voucher. Confound: the June 2026 tranche coincides with the
  energy shock that began on 28 Feb 2026.
- G1 Decision: MOF, Budget 2027 cost-of-living package. Yes: part of the
  voucher's value goes to sellers, which strengthens the case for cash or
  targeted help. No: removes the demand-pull objection. Unclear: too noisy to
  say. Stakes Medium (the June 2026 tranche cost S$700.07m, CNA 11 Jun 2026).
- G2 Live argument: the PQ of 3 Mar 2026; KF Seetoh urging hawkers to raise
  prices (The Online Citizen, 18 Mar 2026); heartland retailers asking for CDC
  vouchers to continue (CNA, 18 Jun 2026). Timing: the pre-Budget window
  (MOF opened Budget 2026 views on 2 Dec 2025, so about 2 Dec 2026 by precedent).
- G3 Headlines: yes, "Hawker prices rose the month vouchers landed"; no,
  "Vouchers landed. Hawker prices did not jump" (sides with MOF, on a test MOF
  did not run). Both are news: the belief is common, and MOF's evidence was
  annual.
- G4 Who it serves: every household that spends the vouchers; MOF (Budget 2027)
  and the CDCs; channel: REACH pre-Budget consultation, and an answer to the
  MP's follow-up ("Has the Government conducted any studies ... ?").
- G5 Cost: 3-4 working days (one table, about ten event months). Fits the
  pre-Budget slot of about 2 Dec 2026.

**Overlap.** Method overlaps the GST pass-through piece (price steps at dated
events); the question is new.

**Borrowed.** Placebo months, as in a permutation test: compare each tranche
month with the same calendar month in every non-tranche year, so the result
is a rank, not a model. (Same logic as the GST piece's no-tax Januaries.)

**Seal note.** No monthly hawker comparison found in any source. MOF's reply
used annual all-items figures only.

## 3. AI and fresh graduates: is the bottom rung breaking?

**Claim, as people say it.** "We are concerned that there may be that bottom
rung of the career ladder that is broken, and young people may not get
sufficient training." PM Lawrence Wong, DBS Asia Future Forward conference,
12 Oct 2026 (CNA, https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/lawrence-wong-ai-jobs-training-6452176).
The rival view: "the specific impact of AI on the demand for entry-level jobs
remains uncertain at this point" (MOE in Parliament, 6 Apr 2026,
https://telescope.gov.sg/transcript/28978); AI is "replacing certain tasks, but
not entire jobs" (Ong Ye Kung, 7 Sep 2026, CNA).

**Decision.** PM Wong: "we are looking at whether there is a need for new
programmes, new schemes" (ST, 12 Oct 2026), most likely in Budget 2027 and the
Committee of Supply (Feb-Mar 2027). MOE has extended the GRIT traineeships to
the 2026 cohort and is "prepared to expand capacity if needed" (6 Apr 2026).
Readers decide too: students choosing courses in the 2027 university round.

**Testable question.** Are graduates of the courses AI can do losing more
ground than the rest?

**Data.**
- data.gov.sg d_3c55210de27fcccda2ed0c63fdd2b352, "Graduate Employment Survey -
  NTU, NUS, SIT, SMU, SUSS & SUTD" (MOE), annual, coverage 2013-2025, updated
  5 Mar 2026. Columns: year, university, school, degree,
  employment_rate_overall, employment_rate_ft_perm, basic and gross monthly
  mean, median and percentiles.
- The 2026 survey: fieldwork about Nov 2026, publication about Mar 2027 (the
  2025 survey was published in March 2026, per WSG's fresh-graduate page).
- AI exposure by occupation: Eloundou et al., "GPTs are GPTs"
  (https://github.com/openai/GPTs-are-GPTs), mapped to degree groups by a rule
  written into THESIS before any GES value is opened.
- MOM entry-level PMET vacancy counts, as given in Parliament (4 Aug 2026).

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: partly. Degree-level employment rates exist; the exposure map
  is a judgement call and the weak point. Outcome-neutral check: degrees that
  lead to licensed jobs (medicine, nursing, law) should show high, stable
  rates; if the design flags them, it is broken.
- G1 Decision: MOF and MOM on "new schemes" (Budget 2027); MOE intake
  planning; students' course choices. Yes: aim support at graduates of exposed
  courses and back training requirements on employers. No: points to the
  hiring cycle, the MOE view. Stakes High.
- G2 Live argument: PM 12 Oct 2026; ST 1 Nov 2026; Business Times 19 Oct 2026;
  CNA 14 Aug 2026; PQs of 3 Feb and 4 Aug 2026. Timing: Budget 2027, then the
  2026 survey in about March 2027.
- G3 Headlines: yes, "AI is shutting graduates out of the jobs it can do"
  (confirms the PM's worry); no, "Graduates in AI's path did no worse"
  (myth-busting a belief stated at the top).
- G4 Who it serves: students aged 17-24 and their parents; MOE and MOM (units
  not verified); channel: Budget 2027 and Committee of Supply debates.
- G5 Cost: 8-10 working days, mostly the exposure map. Displaces the menu's
  graduate starting-pay candidate, or merges with it: same dataset, one seal.

**Overlap.** Partial: the menu candidate "graduate starting pay in CPI, hawker
and flat terms" uses the same GES dataset. This asks a different question (jobs
by exposure, not pay); propose as an extension or a merged piece.

**Borrowed.** Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six
Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence"
(Stanford Digital Economy Lab, Aug 2025, updated Aug 2026,
https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/):
young workers in high- and low-exposure jobs, compared as a gap from a shared
path, not as levels.

**Seal note.** Overall GES employment rates for 2023-2025 are public and quoted
by ministers and the press; a 2025 fall in one course cluster was named in
Parliament (6 Apr 2026). The sealed bet must be on the 2026 survey and on the
exposure ranking, not on the overall rate.

## 4. The RTS Link and a cheap ringgit: do Singapore's supermarkets lose?

**Claim, as people say it.** "When the train opens, we're doomed." (a Woodlands
retailer, CNA TODAY Big Read, 7 Aug 2026,
https://www.channelnewsasia.com/today/big-read/johor-bahru-rts-link-singapore-business-shopping-lower-prices-6303161).
A study by the Singapore Business Federation, Restaurant Association of
Singapore and Singapore Retailers Association projects a net outflow of about
0.4 per cent of Singapore's retail and F&B sales once the line opens, groceries
first (CNA, 16 Jul 2026,
https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/singapore-spend-more-jb-rts-link-opens-6257241).
Rivals: OCBC says the RTS Link is "not expected to topple" Singapore retail
(Business Times, 10 Apr 2026); an academic told Singapore Business Review that
the exchange rate "could have a greater impact on cross-border spending than
the RTS Link itself" (SBR, 24 Sep 2026).

**Decision.** Passenger service starts early 2027: February 2027 per Mothership
(4 Oct 2026), January 2027 per earlier reports; no official date seen. Budget
2027 heartland support: Woodlands merchants asked for rental support and for
CDC vouchers to continue (CNA, 18 Jun 2026). HDB is landlord to most heartland
shops.

**Testable question.** When the ringgit is cheap, do Singapore's supermarkets
sell less? (A second sealed bet in the same piece: do supermarket sales dip,
against trend, after the RTS Link opens?)

**Data.**
- SingStat M602201, "Retail Sales Index, (2025 = 100), In Chained Volume Terms,
  Monthly", 1985 Jan-2026 Aug. Rows include Supermarkets & Hypermarkets;
  Mini-Marts & Convenience Stores; Food & Alcohol; Cosmetics, Toiletries &
  Medical Goods; Petrol Service Stations.
- SingStat M602211, "Food & Beverage Services Index, (2025 = 100), In Chained
  Volume Terms, Monthly", 1985 Jan-2026 Aug. Rows: Restaurants; Fast Food
  Outlets; Food Caterers; Cafes; Food Courts & Other Eating Places.
- SingStat M700051, "Exchange Rates (Average For Period), Monthly",
  1988 Jan-2026 Sep, row "Malaysian Ringgit (Singapore Dollar Per Malaysian
  Ringgit)".
- Gap: SingStat M650661, "Outbound Departures Of Singapore Residents By Mode Of
  Transport, Monthly" (2011 Jan-2026 Sep), has Air and Sea only. No public land
  crossing series was found.
- A natural experiment with a confound: the land border closed in March 2020
  and reopened on 1 Apr 2022, alongside COVID rules.

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: partly. National monthly volumes against a monthly exchange
  rate can be tested; the Woodlands-only fear cannot (no regional retail
  series). Outcome-neutral check: petrol station volumes should respond to the
  ringgit if the method can see cross-border buying at all.
- G1 Decision: Budget 2027 heartland support (MOF, MTI and Enterprise
  Singapore, units not verified); HDB rental support. Yes: supports help aimed
  at cross-border competition in groceries. No: says merchants' costs, not
  Johor, are the problem. Stakes Medium.
- G2 Live argument: CNA 18 Jun, 16 Jul, 23 Jul and 7 Aug 2026; ST 13 Jul 2026;
  SBR 24 Sep 2026. Timing: the RTS opening, early 2027.
- G3 Headlines: yes, "A cheap ringgit empties Singapore's supermarkets"
  (confirms the merchants); no, "Even a cheap ringgit barely moves groceries"
  (myth-busting, against a named industry forecast).
- G4 Who it serves: readers on the weekend JB run and heartland shop owners;
  the trade bodies that made the forecast; channel: Budget 2027 consultation,
  news peg on opening day.
- G5 Cost: 6-8 working days.

**Overlap.** New. Shares the exchange-rate theme with the Singapore dollar and
yen piece; different currency, different outcome.

**Borrowed.** Baggs, Fung and Lapham (2018), "Exchange rates, cross-border
travel, and retailers: Theory and empirics", Journal of International
Economics 115 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/inecon/v115y2018icp59-79.html): a
stronger Canadian dollar raised cross-border trips and cut Canadian retailers'
sales, more so near the border. Also: score a named outside forecast (the
industry study) alongside the site's own sealed bet.

**Seal note.** No supermarket-versus-ringgit result seen. The industry figure
is a projection, not an outcome.

## 5. The 10-cent deposit: will six in ten bottles and cans come back?

**Claim, as people say it.** "It would also be useful for the public to know
whether the scheme has resulted in a meaningful increase in recycling rates,
and whether the environmental benefits outweigh the additional infrastructure
and operating resources required." (Raymond Lim, ST Forum, 30 Sep 2026,
https://www.straitstimes.com/opinion/forum/forum-is-drink-container-return-scheme-a-boost-for-recycling-or-side-hustle-income).
Also "Beverage Container Return Scheme may be more trouble than it's worth"
(Cheong Wing Kiat, ST Forum, 31 Jul 2026). The rival view, NEA's reply: "The
early results are encouraging." (ST Forum, 9 Oct 2026,
https://www.straitstimes.com/opinion/forum/forum-early-results-of-beverage-container-return-scheme-encouraging).
The operator "did not state ... the return rate" (CNA, 21 Sep 2026).

**Decision.** The first compliance year ends 31 Mar 2027. BCRS Ltd must report
to NEA within three months of each year's end (Senior Minister of State Janil
Puthucheary, Parliament, 5 Aug 2026). Targets: at least 60 per cent in year
one, 80 per cent from 2029 (CNA, 21 Sep 2026). NEA: "These insights will help
us improve the scheme" (9 Oct 2026): deposit level, return points, scope.

**Testable question.** Will the 10-cent deposit bring back six in ten drink
containers in its first year?

**Data.**
- BCRS Ltd's first annual report to NEA, due by about 30 Jun 2027. Whether the
  return rate will be published is not confirmed (gap).
- SingStat M891371, "Waste Management And Overall Recycling Rates, Annual"
  (also data.gov.sg d_daf568968ab40dc81e7b08887a83c8fa), 2000-2025, updated
  21 Jul 2026; rows include Plastic, Ferrous Metal, Non-Ferrous Metal. The 2026
  figures are due about mid-2027.
- Interim container counts (not rates) given in Parliament (5 Aug 2026), by
  BCRS Ltd (21 Sep 2026) and by NEA (9 Oct 2026).
- Reference class: Reloop, Global Deposit Book 2024 and its Nov 2025 fact sheet
  on first-year results of new schemes.

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: yes if the operator publishes its rate; otherwise only the
  fallback, NEA's 2026 tonnages, which cover a calendar year with nine months
  of the scheme. Outcome-neutral check: units sold must be reported, or there
  is no rate to score.
- G1 Decision: NEA and BCRS Ltd's review of deposit level and return points.
  Yes: the design stands. No: a case for a higher deposit or more return points
  (Reloop finds higher deposits go with higher return rates). Stakes
  Low-Medium.
- G2 Live argument: ST Forum letters of 22 Jan, 5 Feb, 23 Mar, 31 Jul and
  30 Sep 2026; NEA reply 9 Oct 2026. Timing: year one ends 31 Mar 2027.
- G3 Headlines: yes, "The 10-cent deposit hit its first-year target" (rebuts
  the sceptical letters); no, "The 10-cent deposit missed its first-year
  target" (confirms them).
- G4 Who it serves: households deciding whether the trip to a machine is worth
  it; NEA; channel: the forum thread and NEA's annual waste statistics.
- G5 Cost: 3-4 working days: one scorable number and a sealed prior from other
  countries. Fills the gap between Budget 2027 and mid-2027.

**Overlap.** New.

**Borrowed.** Reference-class forecasting from Reloop's Global Deposit Book
(https://www.reloopplatform.org/global-deposit-book/): seal a prior built from
other schemes' first-year rates and deposit levels, then score it like any
other prediction.

**Seal note.** Interim container counts are public; no return rate is. The
pre-scheme NEA recycling series is public (see the seal log).

## 6. The new jobless payout: did it slow the return to work?

**Claim, as people say it.** The Government's long-held worry, as summarised by
a commentary: "the Government has traditionally had reservations on
implementing direct financial unemployment support. This position has been
heavily influenced by the concept of moral hazard." (maju.sg, 30 Aug 2026,
https://www.maju.sg/post/the-skillsfuture-jobseeker-support-scheme-one-year-on).
The rival view, NTUC's Patrick Tay in the Budget debate: "By easing financial
pressure, JSS helps workers avoid rushing into poor job matches that lead to
underemployment." (Business Times, 25 Feb 2026,
https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/singapore/budget-2026-raise-jobseeker-support-schemes-s5000-income-cap-cover-more-retrenched-pmes-says-ntuc).
MOM: "It is still too early to draw firm conclusions ... We are studying if
scheme beneficiaries have better employment outcomes compared to similar
jobseekers who do not receive the support." (written answer, 7 Oct 2026,
https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/1007-written-answer-to-pq-on-jss-assessment-and-employment-outcomes).

**Decision.** MOM's evaluation and the income cap: NTUC asked for the S$5,000
cap to rise to S$7,600 (25 Feb 2026); MOM said it would "assess the case for
extending the scheme to higher-earning individuals" (14 Oct 2025, slightly over
12 months old). No date set; Budget 2027 or the Committee of Supply is the
likely moment.

**Testable question.** Did the new jobless payout slow people's return to work?

**Data.**
- SingStat M181571, "Re-Entry Into Employment Rate, Quarterly", 1997 Q1-2026 Q2,
  one row ("Re-Entry Rate Of Residents Made Redundant").
- MOM MRSD annual "Redundancy and Re-entry into Employment" report, with
  re-entry by age, occupation and education (table labels not checked).
- Design: before and after April 2025, for groups mostly under the cap
  (non-PMETs) against groups mostly over it (senior PMEs).

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: weak to medium. Public data are aggregates; the cap's sharp
  edge needs MOM's own records. Outcome-neutral check: re-entry must dip in
  known downturns (2009, 2020).
- G1 Decision: MOM on the cap and the activity rules. Yes: slower re-entry in
  covered groups supports caution on raising the cap. No: weakens the
  moral-hazard objection. Unclear is the likeliest result. Stakes Medium.
- G2 Live argument: Budget debate 25 Feb 2026; CNA 6 Apr 2026; maju.sg
  30 Aug 2026; PQ 7 Oct 2026. Timing: Budget 2027.
- G3 Headlines: yes, "The jobless payout slowed the return to work"; no, "The
  jobless payout slowed no one down" (myth-busting a belief the Government
  itself held for decades).
- G4 Who it serves: readers facing retrenchment; MOM and WSG (being merged with
  SkillsFuture Singapore into a new statutory board, Budget 2026); channel:
  MOM's own evaluation.
- G5 Cost: 5-6 working days, with a high risk of "unclear".

**Overlap.** New.

**Borrowed.** Card, Chetty and Weber (2007), "Cash-on-Hand and Competing Models
of Intertemporal Behavior", QJE 122(4) (https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.2007.122.4.1511):
uses a sharp eligibility line for a payout to measure its effect on job search.
Here, the S$5,000 cap is the line, if MOM's records ever open.

**Seal note.** MOM's 7 Oct 2026 answer published beneficiaries' own placement
figures; no comparison group has been published.

## 7. Flats turning five: do they cool their own town?

**Claim, as people say it.** MND and HDB: the resale market's stabilisation "is
further supported by a larger number of new flats completing their minimum
occupation period (MOP) and entering the resale market over the next few
years" (joint statement, 28 Jul 2026, CNA,
https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/15-month-wait-out-period-removed-hdb-private-chee-hong-tat-6281891).
Earlier, from Minister Chee Hong Tat: "Once we see more supply coming in ...
I think we will see moderation in the resale flat prices in the years ahead"
(Business Times, 28 May 2025; older than 12 months). The rival view: "'Supply
onslaught' to weigh on HDB resale market in 2027, but cash-rich buyers to
support prices" (EdgeProp, 2026,
https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/supply-onslaught-weigh-hdb-resale-market-2027-cash-rich-buyers-support-prices).

**Decision.** MND and HDB on BTO volumes for 2027-2028 and the remaining
cooling measures (the 30-month wait-out for subsidised purchases stays; the
review of singles' eligibility age was mentioned on 28 Jul 2026, ST). The
15-month wait-out was removed on 28 Jul 2026, so that decision is done.

**Testable question.** Do towns where more flats turn five see slower resale
prices?

**Data.**
- data.gov.sg d_8b84c4ee58e3cfc0ece0d773c8ca6abc, "Resale flat prices based on
  registration date from Jan-2017 onwards" (HDB), monthly, Jan 2017-Nov 2026.
  Columns: month, town, flat_type, block, street_name, storey_range,

## 8. Productivity and pay: where output jumped, did pay follow?

**Claim, as people say it.** "We need to repair this lag. Singaporean workers
have kept their end of the bargain. Their compensation has not kept up with it."
(Kenneth Tiong, 10 Aug 2026, https://www.kennethtiong.com/p/an-economy-of-the-future-that-works).
The rival view, MOM: "The Government and our tripartite partners have taken the
long-standing principle that real wage growth should be in line with
productivity growth" (written answer to Mr Melvin Yong, 10 Sep 2026,
https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0910-written-answer-to-pq-on-productivity-and-wage-growth).
And the split itself: "Singapore's economy is growing - why aren't all workers
feeling it?" (CNA, 27 Aug 2026).

**Decision.** The National Wages Council's 2027/2028 guidelines (it convened
August to October in 2026; the 2026/2027 guidelines were accepted on
19 Oct 2026, https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2026/1019-nwc-guidelines-for-2026-2027).

**Testable question.** In the sectors where output per hour grew fastest, did
pay grow fastest too?

**Data.**
- SingStat M015851, "Value Added Per Actual Hour Worked In Chained (2015)
  Dollars By Industry, Annual", 2009-2025 (24 rows, including Total and Food &
  Beverage Services); M015871, the quarterly version, 2017 Q1-2026 Q2.
- SingStat M016011, "Compensation Of Employees By Industry At Current Prices,
  Annual", 1980-2025.
- MOM median gross monthly income by industry (Labour Force in Singapore
  tables; labels not checked); SingStat M920131, median gross monthly income by
  occupation and sex, 2001-2024.

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: yes at sector level. Weak point: shifts in who works in each
  sector.
- G1 Decision: the NWC and MOM. Yes: supports sector-calibrated guidelines, as
  the 2026/2027 round already leans. No: supports national floors (PWM, Local
  Qualifying Salary) over sector bargaining. Stakes Medium.
- G2 Live argument: Tiong 10 Aug 2026; CNA 27 Aug 2026; PQ 10 Sep 2026;
  Atlantic Council report 9 Jan 2026. Timing: NWC 2027/2028.
- G3 Headlines: yes, "Pay followed productivity, sector by sector"; no, "The
  sectors that got more productive did not pay more". The national version is
  now a zero cell (answered by MOM).
- G4 Who it serves: workers in the fast-growing sectors; the NWC; channel: the
  NWC's call for submissions (NTUC, SNEF).
- G5 Cost: 5-6 working days.

**Overlap.** Adjacent to the PWM piece (low pay); new question.

**Borrowed.** Stansbury and Summers (2017), "Productivity and Pay: Is the link
broken?", PIIE working paper 17-4
(https://www.piie.com/publications/working-papers/productivity-and-pay-link-broken):
the gap depends on mean versus median pay and on the price index used, which
is the "where the finding flips" section (STORY_CRAFT S6) waiting to be written.
Presentation to answer: EPI's productivity-pay chart
(https://www.epi.org/productivity-pay-gap/).

**Seal note.** MOM's 10 Sep 2026 answer gave both national growth rates for
2015-2025, and MOM's Labour Force advance release (28 Nov 2025) compared income
growth with productivity. Only the sector split can be sealed.

## 9. Cheaper childcare: will more mothers go back to work?

**Claim, as people say it.** "As costs of childcare fall and availability rises,
some parents, especially mothers, may be motivated to return to, or stay in,
the workforce." (UOB's Suan Teck Kin, Business Times, 25 Aug 2026,
https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/singapore/economy-policy/ndr-2026-new-workplace-policies-parents-add-options-labour-market).
The rival view in the same article: "We may see more parents return to work,
but it is unlikely to happen immediately or in very large numbers."

**Decision.** MSF and ECDA: full-day childcare fees at government-supported
preschools fall to S$150 a month and infant care to S$300, "gradually ... from
2028, reaching $150 a month by 2030" (Stomp, 7 Sep 2026, on the National Day
Rally of 23 Aug 2026), with equal subsidies for working and non-working
applicants. 2027 is the design year (inference; no date announced).

**Testable question.** When childcare got cheaper before, did more mothers of
young children work?

**Data.**
- SingStat M182201 (participation by age and sex, 1990-2025).
- MOM tables on residents outside the labour force by main reason (family
  responsibilities, childcare); labels not checked.
- ECDA's fee caps for anchor and partner operators by year: not compiled; no
  childcare row was found in CPI table M213751 (its Education rows stop at
  tuition).

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: weak. No public series for mothers by age of youngest child
  was found, and fee caps moved in small steps. Stop risk.
- G1 Decision: MSF and ECDA sequencing the 2028-2030 cuts. Stakes Medium.
- G2 Live argument: Business Times 25 Aug 2026; Stomp 7 Sep 2026. Timing:
  design in 2027, cuts from 2028.
- G3 Headlines: yes, "Cheaper childcare brought mothers back to work"; no,
  "Cheaper childcare did not bring mothers back".
- G4 Who it serves: parents of under-sevens; MSF and ECDA; channel: MSF's
  Committee of Supply 2027.
- G5 Cost: 6-8 working days, mostly compiling fee history.

**Overlap.** New.

**Borrowed.** Baker, Gruber and Milligan (2008), "Universal Child Care, Maternal
Labor Supply, and Family Well-Being", Journal of Political Economy 116(4)
(https://doi.org/10.1086/591908), on Quebec's $5-a-day childcare.

**Seal note.** None seen.

## 10. The carbon tax: did it reach power bills in full?

**Claim, as people say it.** The Government's own estimate: "On average, with
every $5/tCO2e increase in the carbon tax level, electricity tariffs could rise
by around 1%", about $3 a month for an average four-room flat at $45 (NCCS,
https://www.nccs.gov.sg/singapores-climate-action/mitigation-efforts/carbontax/;
ST, 22 Jan 2026). On the next step: the 2030 rate "could be on the lower end of
the $50 to $80 range" (PM Wong, Budget 2026, ST 12 Feb 2026).

**Decision.** The carbon tax path after 2027 (MSE and MOF): the Government
"will provide updates in due course" (MSE written reply, 24 Sep 2025, older than
12 months). The 2028 rate must be set before 2028, so Budget 2027 is the likely
moment (inference).

**Testable question.** Did the 2024 carbon tax rise reach household power bills
one for one?

**Data.**
- SP Group quarterly tariff releases, 2019-2026, with the four components
  (energy costs, network costs, market support services fee, market
  administration and power system operation fee); e.g.
  https://www.spgroup.com.sg/about-us/media-resources/news-and-media-releases/Electricity-Tariff-Revision-for-the-Period-1-July-to-30-September-2026.
- SingStat M213751, row 1.03.2.3 "Electricity", monthly.
- EMA Singapore Energy Statistics tariff tables and EMC wholesale prices: not
  checked.

**Gates.**
- G0 Answerable: weak. The energy-cost component folds fuel and carbon
  together, and the 2026 step coincides with the 2026 energy shock. The 2024
  step is cleaner.
- G1 Decision: MSE and MOF on the 2028-2030 path and on the size of U-Save.
  Stakes Medium.
- G2 Live argument: ST 22 Jan and 12 Feb 2026. Timing: Budget 2027
  (inference).
- G3 Headlines: yes, "The carbon tax reached power bills in full"; no, "Power
  bills rose less than the carbon tax implied". Zero-cell risk: the official
  estimate already assumes full pass-through, so "yes" is little news.
- G4 Who it serves: households; NCCS and MSE; channel: Budget 2027.
- G5 Cost: 5-7 working days.

**Overlap.** An extension of the GST pass-through piece (did the tax reach the
bill?).

**Borrowed.** Fabra and Reguant (2014), "Pass-Through of Emissions Costs in
Electricity Markets", American Economic Review 104(9)
(https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.104.9.2872).

**Seal note.** No pass-through estimate seen beyond the Government's own.

## Inspiration: methods and presentation worth borrowing

- **FiveThirtyEight, "Checking our work"**
  (https://projects.fivethirtyeight.com/checking-our-work/): a calibration
  chart that bins every forecast by its stated probability and shows how often
  each bin came true. The scorecard already reports a Brier score; once there
  are enough confidence-tagged predictions, this chart is the readable version.
- **FiveThirtyEight's data repository** (github.com/fivethirtyeight/data): one
  folder per article, with the data and a README. The piece repos
  (coe-analysis and others) already do this; a one-line README pointer from
  each answer article would make it visible to readers.
- **Our World in Data** (https://ourworldindata.org/faqs; pipeline at
  github.com/owid/etl): every chart carries its source and retrieval date in
  the caption. Borrow for captions (STORY_CRAFT S8): table id plus retrieval
  date under every chart, not only in raw/RETRIEVED.txt.
- **AsPredicted** (https://aspredicted.org/): a short pre-registration form
  (main question, outcome measure, analyses, exclusions, sample). A checklist
  for THESIS.md, and a third-party timestamp in addition to the git commit.
- **OSF Preregistration** (https://osf.io/prereg/) and **Registered Reports**
  (https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports): an external, frozen copy
  of a seal; the Registered Report format is already the model for the paper
  track in office/PURPOSE_PRINCIPLES.md.
- **Open Hansard** (https://openhansard.sg/; code at github.com/roycehoe/hansard,
  "Singapore's parliamentary record, split into speeches and linked to the
  people who gave them"): the fastest way found to collect dated live arguments
  for G2 and the question trail behind each decision. Used for candidate 2.
- **A data.gov.sg wrapper** (github.com/yuhui/datagovsg) and the two metadata
  endpoints used in this scout (SingStat `/api/table/metadata/<id>`, data.gov.sg
  `/v2/public/api/datasets/<id>/metadata`): both return labels and coverage
  without values, which is exactly what a `00_coverage.py` needs before a seal.
- **Reference-class priors** (Reloop's Global Deposit Dashboard,
  https://www.reloopplatform.org/global-deposit-dashboard/): seal a prior from
  how other places fared, then score it. Applies beyond candidate 5 (for
  example to retirement-age steps abroad for candidate 1).
- **Score an outside forecast.** Candidate 4 has a named industry forecast and
  a named bank view. Sealing the site's bet next to theirs makes "us versus
  them" visible on the scorecard without the site taking a side in advance.
